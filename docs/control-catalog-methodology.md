# AAGRA GAAF 1.1 Control Catalogue Methodology

The GAAF 1.1 baseline contains 1,000 structured controls: 20 domains with 50 controls per domain. Each control has a stable code, objective, evidence expectation, applicability statement, lifecycle stage, control type, criticality and framework source reference. Existing assessment records remain versioned against the methodology version that created them.

The catalogue is intentionally a practical assessment baseline rather than a certification checklist. Controls are phrased as reviewable outcomes, with evidence expectations that can be adapted to the entity type, jurisdiction, sector, AI use case and risk level. A control can be marked not applicable only through an explicit applicability decision and documented rationale in a future review workflow.

The content architecture is aligned at a high level with NIST AI RMF’s Govern, Map, Measure and Manage functions and its lifecycle-oriented risk management approach. It also reflects ISO/IEC 42001’s AI management system perspective: policy, roles, planning, operation, evaluation and continual improvement. For regulated or high-risk deployments, the catalogue includes control families that can be mapped to requirements such as human oversight, representative input data, operational monitoring, logging, incident reporting and cooperation with authorities. The platform does not claim legal compliance or certification from these mappings.

## Source references

[1]: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework "NIST AI Risk Management Framework"
[2]: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/ "NIST AI RMF Core"
[3]: https://www.iso.org/standard/42001 "ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Artificial intelligence management system"
[4]: https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26 "EU AI Act Article 26: Obligations of deployers of high-risk AI systems"

NIST describes the AI RMF as voluntary guidance for managing risks to individuals, organizations and society, and organizes its Core into Govern, Map, Measure and Manage functions [1] [2]. ISO describes ISO/IEC 42001 as requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an AI management system [3]. The EU AI Act service desk lists deployer obligations including competent human oversight, input-data management, monitoring, logging and incident escalation for high-risk AI systems [4].
